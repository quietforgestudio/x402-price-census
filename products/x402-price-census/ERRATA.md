## 2026-11-06 — the report dated our own prose warning six days too early, and in the wrong direction

**Found by our own pre-publication review of a forum post repeating the same sentence**, one day
after `SCHEME_CENSUS.md` was published. No external reader reported it. The figures were not
affected: every count in the scheme census is unchanged and still re-derivable from
`scheme_evidence.json`.

### What the sentence said

> Our index page had warned about this shape in prose since 2026-10-30, on three hand-checked
> rows; what did not exist was a machine-readable field or a count.

(in `SCHEME_CENSUS.md` under the silent-tie bullet, and a second copy in `README.md`.)

### Why that is wrong

Nothing on 2026-10-30 touched this shape. The index page's prose note about scheme ties was
written on **2026-11-05**, hours before the census, and the `pay_basis` / `pay_alternatives`
fields it talks about shipped the same day. Worse than the six-day error: **the clause that note
replaced said the opposite.** The page had explained the missing "1 of N" mark as a route that
"declared the same option twice ... there is nothing to disambiguate" — which was wrong on **7 of
the 8** rows it covered, where the two options differ in `scheme` and there is very much something
to disambiguate.

So the original sentence credited us with a standing warning we did not have, and omitted that the
page had been misleading on exactly this point until the day before. That is self-flattering in
the one direction a reader cannot check from the published files.

### What changed

Both copies now read: the page "got a prose note about this shape only on 2026-11-05, hours before
this census, off three hand-checked served 402s — and the clause it replaced said the opposite ...
which was wrong on 7 of the 8 rows it covered."

The sentence is generated by one tool (`bin/x402_scheme_census.py`), so the report was regenerated
from the same frozen capture rather than hand-edited; the regenerated file differs from the
published one in exactly three lines — its own timestamp and the two corrected sentences.

`verify_scheme_census.py` gained **six legs** that pin the correction in *both* documents (the
string `2026-10-30` must be absent, the 2026-11-05 dating present, and the "wrong on 7 of the 8
rows" admission present), each demonstrated to go red on the defect it describes. A figure with no
leg is how this one survived publication: the verifier checked every number in both copies and no
sentence.

### Also corrected in the same pass

`README.md` claimed the verifier "re-derives every published figure from the evidence rows". It
does not, and cannot: the three population figures (6,016 fetched rows, 54,489 declared, the 11.0%
share) describe the capture, while the evidence rows are the 5,994 de-duplicated routes. Those
three are **cross-checked between documents**, never derived. The README now says so, and two more
verifier legs pin that too.

## 2026-11-04 — 13 rows asserted `payto_equal: true` on payees the same rows called not comparable

**Found by re-reading our own classifier**, after an external reviewer asked how this table
handles a live challenge that offers several `accepts[]` entries. The question arrived in private
correspondence, so nothing of theirs is quoted, characterised or relied on here: the answer to
their actual question turned out to be "not the way you think", and checking it is what surfaced a
different defect of ours underneath. **Every number below is reproducible from the published files
alone.**

### What the rows said

> `"payto_comparable": false, "payto_equal": true`

on 9 routes of `47620.xyz` and 4 of `mpp.hyreagent.fun` — 13 of 828 rows.

### Why that is wrong

Every one of those 13 payees is a 44-character **base58 Solana** address that the upstream capture
had already case-folded. `x402_route_table.py`'s own module documentation states the rule:

> Folding is lossless for EVM addresses (EIP-55 checksum is only casing) and LOSSY for base58
> Solana addresses, which are case-sensitive, so a folded base58 payee is reported with
> `payto_comparable: false` **rather than asserted equal**.

The code set the flag and then asserted the equality anyway. `ROUTE_LAYER.md`'s limit 3 has always
disclosed the folding in prose, so a reader of the document was not misled, and the qualifier sat
two fields away in the same row object. **The defect is therefore a self-contradictory row rather
than an unqualified claim** — and that is still worth fixing, because a program cannot tell which
of two disagreeing fields wins: one reading `payto_equal` without also reading `payto_comparable`
got a bare `true`.

### What changed

`payto_equal` is now `null` wherever `payto_comparable` is not `true`. **Exactly 13 cells changed,
all `true` → `null`**, and every aggregate is byte-identical with no verdict moved. **Nothing is
lost:** `declared_payto` and `live_payto` are still in every row verbatim, so a reader who wants
the case-folded comparison can fold them.

**9 `verdict_reason` strings also changed, and for the same reason the fields did.** Those rows
said "declared and live amount agree and **no other term disagrees**" while the same row declined
to compare the payee. "No other term" is a claim about the payee too, so on a withheld row it
asserted agreement on a field the row itself will not compare — the identical flag-versus-value
contradiction, surviving in the prose after it was removed from the fields. Those rows now name the
payee as not compared. One transition, 9 rows, nothing else touched.

### Why it survived two prior corrections of this same file

* The verdict came from one named function, but `payto_equal` was assembled **inline at two
  separate call sites**. A rule added to one would have left the other asserting. Both now go
  through a single `payto_claim()`.
* The selftest already had a leg for this rule — `"base58 payTo is NOT case-comparable"` — and it
  asserted `payto_comparable`, **never `payto_equal`**. One leg, half the surface.
* `--reanalyse` recomputes the aggregates **from** the stored rows, so it can only catch a stated
  total that disagrees with the rows beneath it. It never re-ran the classifier, which left every
  per-row derived field an **ungraded** surface: a wrong row-level claim keeps the aggregates
  perfectly consistent. The new `--recheck` re-runs the classifier over every row, fails on any
  disagreement, and prints the population it compared.

### A second, latent defect fixed in the same pass — with no victim in this population

`accepts[]` pairability was gated on the challenge spanning more than one **network**, not on its
**entry count**. A challenge whose entries share the declared network therefore fell straight
through to the scalar amount comparison, which could publish `DIVERGENT` about a host that offers
the declared price at a different entry of the same challenge. The gate is now a named predicate,
`declared_entry_identified()`: the stored network set is a *distinct set*, so it identifies which
entry the scalar came from only when `len(set(live_networks)) == live_accepts_n` — true of
`47620.xyz` (3 entries / 3 networks), false of `saylorinnovations.com` (6 entries / 4 networks).

**On this population 0 rows take the new withholding path**, and that is stated rather than
glossed: the fix is a correctness bound, not a restatement of any published number. The shape is
not hypothetical, though — `saylorinnovations.com` is already in the table with 6 entries behind 4
networks, and it was kept out of the scalar comparison only because its declared network happened
not to match the scalar reading.

An amount **divergence** is still asserted when the declared amount appears in **no** entry of the
observed amount set: a deduped set is exact for *membership* even though it is lossy for the entry
*mapping*, and those two facts license different claims. **Two conditions bound that exculpation
and are now stated in the published `limits`:** the membership test is exact *string* equality, so
a declared `10000` against a live `10000.0` or a different unit will not exculpate; and it assumes
the captured amount set is complete, which no field on the row records.

### Two more of the same class, found by the pre-publication review and fixed in the same pass

Neither had a victim in this population, and both are inherited rather than introduced:

* **An unobserved amount set was collapsed to "one distinct amount."** `distinct_amounts` fell back
  to `1` when the live amount set was empty, which let the pairing step publish `DIVERGENT` against
  "the challenge's only amount" while naming an amount set that was never read. It is `None` now
  and the row is withheld.
* **`declared_terms_present` published `false` for terms it had not observed.** A missing declared
  network or an unread live set produced `false` — "the host does not offer the declared term" —
  rather than "not observed". It is **three-state** now: `true`/`false` only when both operands
  were observed, `null` otherwise. The earlier `bool(x) and x in set` form was not a guard; it also
  answered `false`, which is the wrong value rather than a withheld one.

### Verification

* `--selftest`: **53 → 120** offline checks. The new legs cover the three-state entry predicate,
  the membership exculpation, both payee call sites, the three-state `declared_terms_present`, and
  the unobserved-amount-set branch.
* `--recheck declared_vs_live.json`: **rc=1, naming the 13 rows** before the repair; **rc=0, 828 of
  828 rows compared, 0 disagreeing** after. Both directions were run.
* **Thirteen single-line source mutations** through `bin/mutate_check.py`: all thirteen bite and
  every control returns to the baseline in force (100/100, then 120/120 after the review fixes).
  One surfaces as a `KeyError` rather than a named failure, which is a weaker signal and is
  recorded as such.
* **Monotonicity proven by enumeration rather than argued.** A repair to a census of named third
  parties may *withdraw* an accusation and must never manufacture one. 518,400 field combinations
  run through both the old and the new classifier give **34,074 accusations withdrawn, 0 added**
  (an "accusation" being a `DIVERGENT` verdict or `payto_equal: false`).
* **Three mutations did not bite when first run, and that is what changed the work.** Deleting the
  payee gate left the suite fully green — proving the two legs written for it were withheld by a
  different mechanism (an address-shape check, and a branch that returns before the gate is
  reached), so a fixture that actually reaches it was added. Rewriting `--recheck` to read each
  row's own verdict instead of its `v1_verdict` also left the suite green, because the grader was
  itself ungraded; it has legs now. And a claim in one new docstring — that a bool would satisfy
  `n > 1` — is simply false (`True == 1`, so `True > 1` is already `False`), which made the clause
  it justified dead code. It was deleted rather than kept with a wrong reason.

## 2026-11-02 — "22 of 68 carry a normative amount field" does not mean what the README said it meant

**Found by an independent third reader**, `scavenger-osan`, who identifies as a different
operator, while spot-checking this census. Confirmed here from our own files
before publishing this note.

### What the README claimed

> Only **22 of those 68** carry a normative x402 amount field (`accepts[].amount` or
> `accepts[].maxAmountRequired`, at any nesting depth).

### What the tool actually did

`is_normative()` tested the **leaf key name alone**:

```python
NORMATIVE_LEAVES = {"amount", "maxamountrequired", "maxamount"}

def is_normative(gpath):
    return gpath.rsplit(".", 1)[-1].lower() in NORMATIVE_LEAVES
```

It never required an `accepts[]` ancestor, and it admitted a third leaf, `maxAmount`, that the
README does not name. So any key path ending in `amount` counted — including a **response-example
payload**.

### How much it matters

| | window 1 (2026-10-24) | window 2 (2026-10-31) |
|---|---|---|
| hosts counted by the published `is_normative()` | **22 of 68** | **22 of 67** |
| hosts carrying the README's *stated* rule (`amount` or `maxAmountRequired` under `accepts[]`) | **15 of 68** | **16 of 67** |

The stricter rule also drops the undocumented third leaf `maxAmount`. On this data that changes
nothing — no host carries an `accepts[]`-anchored `maxAmount` — so the whole difference above is
the missing ancestor.

The 7 (window 1) / 6 (window 2) hosts counted on a leaf name alone:

| host | the path that counted it | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `payai.agentstools.dev` | `services[].output_example.serving_size.amount` (28 paths — 24 response-example, 4 `input`/`queryParams` — **none** under `accepts[]`) | mostly response-example payloads (a nutrition serving size, a token balance); the 4 input paths include an EIP-712 `typed_data.message.details.amount`, which is nearer a price but still not an `accepts[]` offer |
| `agents.daedalusdevelopmentgroup.com` | `resources[].amount`, `resources[].maxAmountRequired` | a real x402 v1 spelling, but **not** under `accepts[]` — see the caveat below |
| `x402.freeq.one` | `services[].amount` | vendor container |
| `api.vibe.airforce` | `resourceDetails[].price.amount` | vendor container |
| `api.premiumrewards.vip` | `offers[].amount` | vendor container |
| `contactcampaigns.com` | `endpoints[].maxAmountRequired` | vendor container |
| `x402.aurelianflo.com` | `endpoints[].payment.amount` | vendor container (window 1 only; this origin served a `308` in window 2) |

### The caveat, and why the fix is additive rather than a swap

`agents.daedalusdevelopmentgroup.com` declares `resources[].maxAmountRequired`. That **is** the
x402 v1 spelling of the payable amount — it is simply not nested under `accepts[]`. Narrowing the
predicate to "must sit under `accepts[]`" therefore drops a row that a client author would
reasonably treat as normative. Neither rule is simply right:

* the leaf-only rule **over**-counts, by admitting payload examples;
* the anchored rule **under**-counts, by rejecting a resource-level v1 field.

So `is_normative()` is **left unchanged**, and every previously published figure stays reproducible
from the published tool. A second predicate, `is_normative_anchored()`, is reported beside it:

```
$ python3 x402_shape_census.py --reanalyse census.json
  hosts with >=1 NORMATIVE amount field: 22 of 68
    of those, carrying the README's rule -- a spec leaf UNDER accepts[]: 15 of 68; the other 7 are counted on a leaf name alone (see ERRATA.md)
```
(the second line is one line of real output, shown unwrapped.)

Run the same command against `census_window2.json` for the window-2 pair. Both numbers are derived
from the published row files; no host was re-probed to produce this note.

### What does NOT change

* **The direction is unchanged, and neither figure is "the truth".** 22 is an upper bound and 15
  a lower bound on how many hosts publish a spec-shaped payable amount; we do not claim either is
  the right number, which is exactly why both are reported. (It is tempting to say the finding
  "gets stronger" because 53 of 68 would then read nothing. That would contradict the caveat
  above: 53 includes `agents.daedalusdevelopmentgroup.com`, which does publish a parseable
  `maxAmountRequired`.)
* The no-price count (**27** / **26**), the distinct-path counts (**218** / **174**) and the
  container counts do not use `is_normative()` at all and are untouched.
* **The label-only count (19) DOES come from `is_normative()`** — `summarise()` builds it as
  "served, no `normative_surfaces`, some `label_surfaces`" — and it is unchanged only because
  `is_normative()` is unchanged. Under the anchored rule it would be **24 of 68**, and two hosts
  (`agents.daedalusdevelopmentgroup.com`, `api.vibe.airforce`) would fall into **no bucket at
  all**, because they carry a price surface and no vendor label. That is the concrete reason the
  anchored rule is published as one extra figure rather than as a re-bucketing of the census.
* `WINDOW2_FINDINGS.md` already disclosed a *different, narrower* taxonomy defect
  (`maxAmount` classified normative while `max_amount` classified a label, affecting one surface
  and no bucket) — so the `maxAmount` leaf was on the record already. **The missing `accepts[]`
  ancestor, which is the whole 22-versus-15 difference, was not disclosed anywhere before
  today.**
